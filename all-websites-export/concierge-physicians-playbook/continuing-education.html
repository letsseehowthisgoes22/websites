Continuing Education for Private Physicians

Professional Development · December 2025

Most physicians complete their training with minimal education in addiction medicine. A 2019 survey published in the Journal of General Internal Medicine found that the median number of hours of addiction medicine education in U.S. medical schools was 11 — spread across four years of medical education. For the concierge physician who encounters substance use disorders with increasing frequency in their UHNW patient population, this gap represents both a clinical risk and a professional development opportunity.

Board Certification Pathways

Addiction Medicine (ABPM)

The American Board of Preventive Medicine (ABPM) offers board certification in addiction medicine. This certification is open to physicians who hold primary board certification in any ABMS-recognized specialty, have completed an ACGME-accredited addiction medicine fellowship or have qualifying practice experience, and pass the addiction medicine certification examination. The certification signals a substantive commitment to addiction medicine and provides a credential recognized by patients, families, and referral sources. For the concierge physician who encounters behavioral health situations regularly, this certification represents the gold standard of competence in the field.

Addiction Psychiatry (ABPN)

The American Board of Psychiatry and Neurology (ABPN) offers a subspecialty certification in addiction psychiatry. This pathway is available only to physicians who are already board-certified in psychiatry and have completed an ACGME-accredited addiction psychiatry fellowship. While this pathway is not accessible to most concierge physicians (who are typically trained in internal medicine or family medicine), understanding the credential helps in evaluating potential referral partners.

The DATA 2000 Waiver (X-Waiver)

Prior to 2023, physicians seeking to prescribe buprenorphine for opioid use disorder were required to obtain a special waiver under the Drug Addiction Treatment Act of 2000. The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023 eliminated this separate waiver requirement, allowing any DEA-registered practitioner to prescribe buprenorphine for opioid use disorder. However, the act also imposed a new training requirement: practitioners who apply for or renew their DEA registration must attest to having completed a total of eight hours of training on treating and managing patients with opioid and other substance use disorders. This training requirement applies to all DEA-registered practitioners, including concierge physicians.

CME Programs

ASAM Fundamentals of Addiction Medicine

The American Society of Addiction Medicine offers a comprehensive continuing education curriculum that ranges from introductory courses to advanced clinical topics. The ASAM Review Course in Addiction Medicine provides preparation for the addiction medicine board examination but also serves as a thorough clinical update for physicians who want a rigorous foundation without pursuing certification. ASAM also offers online learning modules on specific topics including screening and brief intervention, medication-assisted treatment, and the ASAM Criteria for patient assessment and placement.

NIDA Clinical Trials Network Dissemination Library

The National Institute on Drug Abuse's Clinical Trials Network maintains a dissemination library of evidence-based treatment resources, including clinical protocols, training materials, and outcome data from large-scale clinical trials. These resources represent the most current evidence on addiction treatment efficacy and are freely available to clinicians.

SAMHSA's SBIRT Training

The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration provides training in Screening, Brief Intervention, and Referral to Treatment (SBIRT) — a public health approach to identifying individuals with substance use disorders and linking them to treatment. While designed for general medical settings, the SBIRT framework is particularly relevant for concierge physicians who see patients in routine visits where substance use concerns may emerge organically.

Conferences and Professional Events

  • ASAM Annual Conference: The premier event in addiction medicine, featuring clinical updates, research presentations, and workshops. Typically held each spring in a major U.S. city.
  • American Psychiatric Association Annual Meeting: Includes extensive programming on substance use disorders, dual diagnosis, and pharmacotherapy. One of the largest medical conferences in the world.
  • American Academy of Addiction Psychiatry Annual Meeting: A more focused event specifically addressing the intersection of psychiatry and addiction.
  • Heckerling Institute on Estate Planning: While not a medical conference, this annual event is the premier gathering for trust and estate attorneys. Concierge physicians who serve UHNW families may find value in understanding the fiduciary and estate planning dimensions of behavioral health.
  • Family Office Exchange (FOX) Events: FOX hosts conferences and forums for family office professionals. These events provide insight into the priorities and language of the family office community — understanding that can improve a physician's ability to communicate with the professional advisors who surround their UHNW patients.

Peer Learning and Case Consultation

For concierge physicians, peer consultation is often more valuable than formal CME. The isolation of private practice — particularly the isolation of managing sensitive behavioral health situations that cannot be discussed with colleagues — can impair clinical judgment and lead to burnout. Structured peer consultation, whether through formal consultation groups or informal collegial relationships, provides clinical perspective, emotional support, and a check on decision-making.

The American Academy of Private Physicians and similar organizations facilitate networking among concierge practitioners. Regional concierge medicine groups in markets like New York, Los Angeles, Palm Beach, and the San Francisco Bay Area provide opportunities for peer connection. Physicians should also consider establishing consultation relationships with addiction medicine specialists who can provide case-specific guidance when complex behavioral health situations arise in practice.

The Business Case for Behavioral Health Competency: For the concierge physician, behavioral health competency is not merely a clinical imperative — it is a practice differentiator. UHNW families increasingly expect their primary care physician to serve as a health coordinator across all domains, including behavioral health. The physician who can competently screen for substance use disorders, initiate a referral process, coordinate with treatment providers, and support long-term recovery provides a level of comprehensive care that distinguishes their practice and deepens the physician-patient relationship.

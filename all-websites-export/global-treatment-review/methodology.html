Our Review Methodology

Transparency about our methods is essential to the credibility of our assessments. The following describes how we evaluate treatment programs and the limitations inherent in our approach.

Evaluation Criteria

Our editorial profiles assess treatment programs across seven dimensions, each chosen for its relevance to families making treatment decisions.

Clinical Leadership. We evaluate the qualifications of the medical director and senior clinical staff. Board certification in addiction medicine (ABPM) or addiction psychiatry (ABPN) is the baseline expectation for medical director qualifications. We also assess the clinical team's depth — the ratio of doctoral-level clinicians to master's-level clinicians, the availability of specialized expertise (trauma therapy, co-occurring disorders, medication management), and the stability of the clinical leadership team.

Therapeutic Modalities. We assess whether the program employs evidence-based therapeutic approaches — cognitive-behavioral therapy, motivational interviewing, contingency management, medication-assisted treatment — and how these modalities are integrated into the treatment program. We note the availability of specialized modalities (EMDR, somatic experiencing, dialectical behavior therapy) that may be relevant for specific clinical presentations.

Individualization. We evaluate whether the program demonstrates genuine individualization of treatment — whether treatment plans are developed based on comprehensive assessment, modified based on patient progress, and responsive to the specific clinical, psychological, and practical needs of the individual — or whether the program applies a standardized protocol to all patients.

Accreditation and Regulatory Status. We verify accreditation status with the relevant accrediting body and note the most recent survey date and any conditions or requirements for improvement. We also note the program's state licensing status and compliance history where this information is publicly available.

Aftercare and Continuity. We assess the program's aftercare planning process — whether the program develops a comprehensive post-discharge plan, facilitates referrals to continuing care providers, and provides any post-discharge follow-up or support. Programs that discharge patients without a detailed aftercare plan represent a significant gap in the continuum of care.

Privacy and Confidentiality. We evaluate the program's privacy infrastructure — its compliance with applicable privacy regulations (42 CFR Part 2, HIPAA, GDPR, or relevant national standards), its practical privacy measures (private rooms, confidential intake processes, communication protocols), and its track record regarding confidentiality breaches.

Setting and Environment. While we prioritize clinical factors over amenities, we recognize that the treatment environment is a variable that affects patient engagement and comfort. We note the physical setting, accommodations, and amenity level without allowing these factors to dominate our assessment.

Information Sources

Our assessments draw on publicly available information including accreditation records, regulatory filings, published outcome data, and academic publications; program-provided information including clinical descriptions, staff qualifications, and program literature (which we evaluate critically, recognizing that marketing materials may not fully represent clinical reality); professional knowledge drawn from consultations with addiction medicine professionals, care managers, and others who interact with treatment programs in the course of their professional practice; and published reviews and patient experience data where available, evaluated with awareness of selection bias and the limitations of self-reported outcomes.

What We Do Not Do

We do not accept payment for inclusion, profile content, or favorable assessment. Our editorial independence is absolute — no program can purchase coverage, influence the content of its profile, or preview our assessment before publication. We do not conduct on-site inspections. While we would prefer to evaluate programs through direct observation, the logistical and financial constraints of global on-site evaluation exceed our current capacity. Our assessments are therefore based on the information sources described above rather than first-hand observation. We do not provide treatment recommendations. Our profiles are informational resources, not clinical guidance. The appropriate treatment for any individual depends on their specific clinical presentation, and that determination should be made by qualified clinical professionals, not by an editorial publication.

Limitations: Our assessments are constrained by the information available to us. Programs may have changed their clinical model, leadership, or operational practices since our most recent evaluation. Our assessments reflect a point in time and should be supplemented with current information obtained directly from the program. We encourage families to use our profiles as a starting point for their own evaluation, not as a definitive assessment of program quality.

Corrections and Updates

We are committed to accuracy. Programs or readers who identify factual errors in our profiles should contact us at editor@globaltreatment.review. We will investigate and correct verified errors promptly. We also welcome information about significant changes to programs we have profiled — leadership changes, accreditation updates, new clinical programs, or other developments that may affect the accuracy of our assessments.

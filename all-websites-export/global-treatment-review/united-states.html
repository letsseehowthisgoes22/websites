US Residential Programs

The American residential treatment landscape is the most diverse in the world — ranging from nonprofit institutions with decades of clinical research to luxury programs where the amenities rival five-star resorts. Separating clinical substance from marketing polish requires careful evaluation.

The American Treatment Landscape

The United States has more addiction treatment programs than any other country — SAMHSA's National Directory of Drug and Alcohol Abuse Treatment Facilities lists over 14,000 programs. Within this vast landscape, a relatively small number of premium residential programs serve the UHNW market, charging fees that range from $30,000 to $100,000 per month. These programs compete on a combination of clinical reputation, amenity quality, location appeal, and the perceived discretion of their operations.

American treatment programs operate within a regulatory framework that varies significantly by state. At the federal level, treatment programs that receive any form of federal funding (a broad category that includes most programs) are subject to 42 CFR Part 2 confidentiality requirements. At the state level, licensing requirements, staffing ratios, and clinical standards vary considerably. Voluntary accreditation — through the Joint Commission or the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF) — provides an additional layer of quality assurance that families should look for when evaluating programs.

Program Profiles

Silver Springs, Florida

The Guest House Ocala

The Guest House Ocala occupies a distinctive position in the American treatment landscape: a trauma-informed residential program that combines clinical rigor with an environment of uncommon beauty and privacy. Located on a 52-acre private estate in central Florida, the program was purpose-built to address the intersection of emotional trauma and addiction — a clinical focus that reflects the growing body of evidence linking adverse childhood experiences, adult trauma, and substance use disorders.

The clinical model is built around trauma resolution rather than traditional addiction-focused programming. The program employs a range of trauma-specific modalities including EMDR, somatic experiencing, internal family systems therapy, and experiential therapies (equine-assisted therapy, art therapy, and nature-based programming). Individual therapy is emphasized alongside group work, and the psychiatric team manages medications with attention to the complex pharmacological considerations of trauma-informed care.

The Guest House holds Joint Commission accreditation and maintains a clinical staff that includes doctoral-level therapists, board-certified psychiatrists, and specialized nursing staff. The program's physical campus — designed to feel like a private estate rather than an institution — reflects an understanding that the treatment environment itself is a therapeutic variable.

Key Data: Joint Commission accredited. 52-acre private campus. Trauma-informed model. Individual and group therapy. Equine-assisted and experiential therapies. Duration: 35-90 days typical. Cost: $48,000-$96,000/month.
Wickenburg, Arizona

The Meadows

The Meadows has been a landmark in the American treatment landscape for over four decades, developing a proprietary therapeutic model — the Meadows Model — that integrates Pia Mellody's developmental immaturity framework with contemporary trauma resolution techniques. The program's influence on the field has been substantial; many of the concepts now common in trauma-informed addiction treatment (including the relationship between childhood developmental trauma and adult addictive behaviors) were popularized through The Meadows' clinical work and publications.

The program operates on a campus in the Arizona desert, providing residential treatment in a setting that emphasizes connection with nature and distance from the distractions of urban life. The clinical program is intensive — patients participate in individual therapy, group therapy, psychoeducation, and experiential activities on a structured daily schedule. The Meadows offers specialized tracks for specific populations, including professionals, first responders, and individuals with sex and relationship addiction.

Key Data: Joint Commission accredited. Founded 1976. Proprietary Meadows Model (Pia Mellody framework). Specialized tracks for professionals. Duration: 45 days standard. Cost: approximately $44,000/45 days. Campus setting in Arizona desert.
Wernersville, Pennsylvania

Caron Treatment Centers

Caron Treatment Centers represents the large nonprofit model at its best — a multi-campus organization with deep clinical resources, extensive research involvement, and a scale that allows specialized programming across multiple populations. Caron's flagship campus in rural Pennsylvania provides residential treatment in a setting that balances clinical intensity with the beauty of the Berks County countryside. The organization also operates programs in Florida and New York.

Caron's clinical model is firmly evidence-based, incorporating CBT, motivational enhancement therapy, twelve-step facilitation, and medication-assisted treatment where indicated. The organization's executive program is notable: it accommodates the specific needs of professionals who require ongoing connection to their work during treatment, providing secure communication facilities and scheduling flexibility while maintaining the clinical intensity of the residential program. Caron's partnership with the NFL for player treatment and its Young Adult program demonstrate the breadth of its clinical capability.

Key Data: CARF accredited. Nonprofit. Multiple campuses (PA, FL, NY). Executive program. NFL partnership. Research involvement. Duration: 28-90 days. Evidence-based clinical model with MAT.
Center City, Minnesota & Multiple Locations

Hazelden Betty Ford Foundation

Hazelden Betty Ford is the nation's largest nonprofit addiction treatment provider, formed by the 2014 merger of two of America's most historic treatment institutions. The organization operates treatment centers in Minnesota, Oregon, California, Illinois, New York, and Florida, providing a comprehensive continuum of care from residential treatment through extended care and outpatient services.

The organization's clinical model has evolved significantly from its twelve-step origins to incorporate a fully integrated approach that includes evidence-based psychotherapy, medication-assisted treatment, and the emerging science of addiction neurobiology. The Butler Center for Research conducts original research on treatment outcomes and publishes findings that contribute to the broader evidence base for addiction treatment.

Key Data: Joint Commission accredited. Nonprofit. Multiple locations nationwide. Butler Center for Research. Comprehensive continuum of care. Duration: 28-90 days residential. Integrated model with MAT.

Additional Programs of Note

Sierra Tucson (Tucson, AZ) — Joint Commission accredited. Integrative model combining evidence-based therapies with neurofeedback, equine therapy, and other complementary approaches. Known for treating complex co-occurring presentations.

Cirque Lodge (Sundance, UT) — Joint Commission and CARF accredited. Experiential therapy emphasis in a dramatic mountain setting. Dual locations: the Lodge (residential) and Studio (clinical). $30,000-$90,000/month.

Promises Malibu (Malibu, CA) — One of the original Malibu luxury programs. Ocean-view setting with an emphasis on individualized programming and holistic approaches alongside evidence-based therapy.

The Dunes East Hampton (East Hampton, NY) — Small-census luxury program on Long Island's East End. Appeal for patients in the New York metropolitan area seeking geographic proximity. Individualized programming in a residential setting.

Origins Behavioral HealthCare (multiple TX locations) — Recovery-focused model emphasizing the twelve-step framework with clinical support. Known for extended care programming and alumni community.

The Amenity-Clinical Spectrum

The American premium treatment market presents families with a fundamental tension: the programs with the most appealing amenities are not always the programs with the strongest clinical programming, and vice versa. Some programs invest heavily in physical plant, celebrity endorsements, and luxury marketing while providing a clinical program that, upon scrutiny, consists primarily of group therapy, experiential activities, and limited individual therapeutic contact. Other programs invest in clinical staffing, research, and evidence-based programming while maintaining facilities that, while comfortable, do not aspire to resort-level luxury.

The family evaluating programs should prioritize clinical indicators — medical director credentials, therapeutic modalities, individual therapy hours, medication management capability, outcome tracking — over environmental features. A program that provides four hours per week of individual therapy with a doctoral-level therapist in a comfortable but unexceptional facility is likely to produce better clinical outcomes than a program that provides one hour per week of individual therapy with a master's-level counselor in a facility with ocean views and a private chef.

The Sober Companion Industry: A Competitive Analysis

Industry Analysis · December 2025

The sober companion market serving ultra-high-net-worth clients has evolved from an informal network of individuals in recovery who accompanied wealthy clients through early sobriety into a structured industry with established firms, differentiated service models, and significant revenue. This analysis surveys the major participants in the UHNW sober companion market, examining their service models, market positioning, and competitive differentiation.

Market Overview

The UHNW sober companion market is characterized by high barriers to entry (reputation, referral relationships, and demonstrated competence with demanding clientele), premium pricing ($1,500-$3,500 per day for qualified practitioners, with specialized or international engagements commanding higher rates), relationship-driven referral patterns (most placements originate from treatment programs, care managers, interventionists, or concierge physicians rather than from direct consumer search), and a fragmented competitive landscape (no single firm dominates the market nationally, and significant regional variation exists in competitive dynamics).

The market has grown substantially over the past decade, driven by increasing awareness of the post-treatment vulnerability period, the professionalization of care management in UHNW families, and the willingness of families to invest in comprehensive aftercare infrastructure. Precise market size is difficult to estimate given the private nature of the industry, but conversations with industry participants suggest that the premium segment (engagements charging $2,000+ per day) represents several hundred active engagements nationally at any given time.

Major Firms

SilverBell Global

SilverBell Global operates what may be the most extensive international network of recovery professionals in the industry. The firm's model emphasizes global reach — the ability to place credentialed recovery professionals with clients anywhere in the world, including in international settings where local recovery infrastructure may be limited. SilverBell's clinical oversight structure, which includes centralized case management and regular check-ins with deployed companions, distinguishes it from firms that function primarily as staffing agencies. The firm has built strong referral relationships with international treatment programs and global family offices.

The Advocacy Group (TAG)

TAG, led by Lauren Brock in Los Angeles, has carved a distinctive position in the market by combining sober companion services with comprehensive case management. The firm's approach extends beyond traditional companionship to include active coordination with treatment providers, family communication management, and lifestyle restructuring support. TAG has particular strength in the entertainment industry, where Brock's relationships and understanding of industry dynamics provide competitive advantage. The firm's model emphasizes clinical sophistication and the ability to manage complex, multi-dimensional recovery situations.

Hired Power

Hired Power, a family-owned firm with over two decades of operation in Southern California, has established itself through longevity, reliability, and a comprehensive service model that includes safe transport (accompanying clients from crisis to treatment), sober companion placement, and recovery assistance. The firm's long operating history provides a depth of experience and referral relationships that newer entrants cannot easily replicate. Hired Power's positioning emphasizes their established track record and the breadth of their service offering across the intervention-to-recovery continuum.

ALYST Health

ALYST Health occupies a unique position in the market as a provider of Joint Commission-accredited at-home addiction treatment. While not a traditional sober companion firm, ALYST's model incorporates recovery support professionals within a clinical treatment framework that is significantly more intensive than standard sober companion placement. The firm's Joint Commission accreditation represents a credibility marker that traditional sober companion agencies have not pursued. ALYST's market positioning bridges the gap between sober companionship and formal treatment, appealing to families who want clinical-grade care delivered in the home setting.

Spearhead Health

Based in Beverly Hills, Spearhead Health positions itself at the intersection of concierge care management and recovery coaching. The firm's model emphasizes lifestyle integration — helping clients build recovery into their daily routines rather than treating recovery as separate from their normal lives. Spearhead's Beverly Hills base provides geographic proximity to a significant concentration of UHNW clients and the entertainment industry professionals who serve them.

Concierge Care Collective

Concierge Care Collective offers a broad care infrastructure that includes nursing, psychiatric stabilization, and long-term planning alongside recovery companion services. The firm operates in New York, Palm Beach, Beverly Hills, and Aspen — markets with high concentrations of UHNW families. The firm's differentiator is the comprehensiveness of its care model: rather than providing a standalone sober companion, Concierge Care Collective can deploy an entire in-home care team that addresses medical, psychiatric, and recovery needs simultaneously.

Coast Health Consulting

Coast Health Consulting focuses specifically on sober companion placement and care coordination, emphasizing the matching process between companions and clients. The firm's model prioritizes credential verification, personality matching, and ongoing clinical oversight of placed companions. Coast Health's positioning emphasizes quality of placement — the argument that the right companion-client match is the single most important factor in engagement success.

Independent Practitioners: A significant portion of the UHNW sober companion market is served by independent practitioners — experienced professionals who have built personal referral networks through years of practice. Independents like James Haggerty (James Haggerty Recovery) and practitioners affiliated with Sober Services International operate without the overhead of a firm structure, often commanding premium rates based on personal reputation and track record. The independent model offers clients direct access to a specific practitioner but lacks the organizational support, backup coverage, and clinical oversight that established firms provide.

Service Model Differentiation

The competitive landscape reveals several distinct service models. The staffing model, in which the agency primarily functions as a matchmaker between clients and independent companions, with limited ongoing clinical involvement after placement. The managed care model, in which the agency maintains active clinical oversight of the engagement, including regular case reviews, communication with the broader care team, and the ability to adjust or replace the companion based on clinical needs. The integrated treatment model, exemplified by ALYST Health, in which recovery support is embedded within a formal clinical treatment framework. And the comprehensive care model, exemplified by Concierge Care Collective and O'Connor Professional Group, in which recovery support is one component of a broader care infrastructure that addresses medical, psychiatric, and family needs.

The trend in the market is toward greater integration and clinical sophistication. Families and care managers increasingly expect recovery support providers to operate as part of a coordinated care team rather than as independent contractors. Firms that can demonstrate clinical oversight structures, outcome tracking, and seamless integration with treatment programs and medical providers are gaining competitive advantage over firms that offer primarily a staffing function.

Market Dynamics

Several trends are shaping the competitive landscape. The professionalization trend — increasing expectations for credentials, clinical supervision, and outcome accountability — favors established firms with organizational infrastructure over independent practitioners and minimally structured agencies. The integration trend — the movement toward coordinated, multi-disciplinary care — favors firms that can interface effectively with treatment programs, care managers, physicians, and family offices. The geographic expansion trend — UHNW families are increasingly mobile, requiring recovery support that can travel internationally — favors firms with global networks or the operational capacity to deploy practitioners across jurisdictions.

The market also faces challenges. The absence of regulatory standards creates quality variability that can damage the industry's reputation when unqualified practitioners cause harm. The relationship-driven nature of referrals creates barriers for new entrants and can perpetuate referral patterns based on personal relationships rather than clinical quality. And the premium pricing of UHNW services creates a two-tier market in which the quality of recovery support available to the wealthy significantly exceeds what is available to the broader population — a disparity that raises questions about equity and access.
